# Pitch outline: six slides, one per rubric question

The HackOHI/O rubric scores six criteria (Innovation, Curiosity, Communication, Breadth and complexity, Impact,
Navigating challenges), each from the demo, the repository and an interview question. Each slide below answers one of
those questions. Every number carries the file and line it was copied from (`results/`, `data/`, or a module in
`concierge/`); nothing here was typed from memory, and no figure appears that a script in `scripts/` did not write.
Labels: **Observed** = organizer data as-is, **Assumed** = a modeling choice in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`, **Simulated** = the
output of a scenario run, **Measured** = a wall-clock runtime on a named machine.

Line numbers refer to the files as they stand on this branch; `results/benchmark_summary.md`,
`results/scale_probe.md` and `data/demo/examples.json` are regenerated by their scripts, so re-check a line before
quoting it if a script has been re-run.

Diagrams for slides 3 and 4 are in [docs/diagrams.md](diagrams.md).

## Slide 1 · "Repair completed is not lights on" (Innovation: how did you challenge accepted ideas?)

- The accepted dispatch orderings, built as baselines on identical inputs: **report order (FIFO)**, "what a simple
  dispatcher does", and **customers-per-hour**, the customer-benefit greedy that ranks each repair by customers
  downstream ÷ hours (`concierge/scheduling.py`, module docstring). Both ignore that a customer's service returns only
  when *every* damaged zone on their upstream path is repaired.
- What we did differently: Horn's tree-merge ordering on the damaged-zone tree, list-scheduled on K crews, plus an
  adjacent-swap pass on the exact customer-outage-hour objective (`concierge/scheduling.py`, docstring); the
  customer-facing consequence is a message that says the repair nearest you is done hours before your lights return.
- Test, not opinion: 306 instances = 46 circuits × 3 damage sets (1 Observed Feb-2025 set + 2 seeded draws) × that
  circuit's crew counts (`results/benchmark_summary.md:3`). Simulated schedules on Observed topology and labor data.
- Result (Simulated): **51.5 %** fewer customer-outage hours than FIFO (median 55.8 %, win rate 100 %) and **9.6 %**
  fewer than the customers-per-hour heuristic (median 7.3 %, win rate 99 %, worst instance −1.6 %)
  (`results/benchmark_summary.md:9-10`). On the Observed Feb-2025 sets alone: 40.0 % and 10.0 %
  (`results/benchmark_summary.md:17-18`).
- The honest version of the same number: at every circuit's own calibrated default crew count the gain over the
  heuristic is **8.6 %** (`results/benchmark_summary.md:34`); 17.5 % applies only to the 10 circuits whose default
  exceeds one crew (`:35`). The heuristic beat us on 3 of 306 instances (`:40`, listed at `:44-46`). On the 64
  instances small enough for brute force, 60 are at the optimum and the largest gap is 9.15 % (`:51`).

**Speaker notes.** Open with the sentence a customer would hear: "the crew finished the pole on your street at hour 2,
but your power came back at hour 11, because a fuse zone upstream was still being repaired." That is the idea the
accepted orderings cannot express. Then say what the baselines are and that all three policies see identical tasks,
hours and crews, so the comparison is fair (`results/benchmark_summary.md:3`). Quote the 51.5 % / 9.6 % headline and
immediately the 8.6 % at calibrated crews and the 3 losses: judges reward the number *and* the caveat. If asked why
the median gain vs the heuristic is lower than the mean (7.3 % vs 9.6 %), the distribution is skewed by a few circuits
where AND-coupling matters a lot; the win rate (99 %) is the robust statement. If asked whether this is optimal: with
one crew and upstream-first precedence Horn's order is optimal; with several crews it is a list-scheduling heuristic
and we say so (`results/benchmark_summary.md:52`). The "what utilities do today" framing is sourced in the README
(AEP Ohio's *Outages FAQ* and *What To Do When The Lights Go Out*, the EEI/DOE restoration steps, the NJ BPU Isaias
report and the Michigan PSC 2026 investigation on inaccurate ETRs); the customers-per-hour baseline is our construction
of the "most customers first" rule those sources describe.

## Slide 2 · Questions we asked of the data (Curiosity: examples of questions; something clever)

- **"Does the upline file key on `id` or on the premise number?"** On `premise_nu`: 117,595 of 117,642 premises have a
  path, 99.96 % (`data/data_report.md:30-33`, Observed); the 47 without one use the circuit-average fallback with Low
  confidence (`ASSUMPTIONS.md`, A1).
- **"Why do recloser tokens never join?"** Matching path tokens to the recloser layer by id matched 0.0 % of 322
  tokens; by location, 100.0 % (`data/data_report.md:41-43`). Fuses join by id (98.51 %, `:39`). **"Is `report_len`
  feet or metres?"** Median ratio to the geodesic length in feet is 0.9971 (`:48`).
- **"Why does the damage file's circuit key not equal `circuit_id`?"** The anonymized id differs between layers, so
  every layer is remapped to the transformer layer's (station, circuit label) key: 14,397 primary and 20,553
  secondary rows moved (`data/data_report.md:62`), 0 damage keys left unresolved (`:83`), 100.0 % of damage rows get a
  circuit and 99.58 % a zone (`:79-80`). **"Are repair dates outage durations?"** No: day granularity, 24 placeholder
  dates nulled (`:54`), never used as durations (`:95`).
- **"Do circuit features predict storm damage better than overhead miles alone?"** Barely. Leave-one-circuit-out
  Spearman 0.658 for the feature model vs 0.644 for exposure only; MAE 8.83 vs 8.63 records per circuit
  (`results/risk_validation.md:27-28`). Damage was concentrated (negative-binomial α_line = 0.119, `:19`; top
  predicted quintile 442.6 predicted vs 655 observed, `:38`), so the zone's own Feb-2025 count carries the estimate
  through empirical-Bayes shrinkage, and the app says so.
- **Something clever:** every protective device in a path is a zone; 7,190 zones form one tree per circuit
  (`data/data_report.md:23-24`), and Horn's tree-merge turns "which zone first" into a provably good order for one
  crew. The piece we are proudest of is the segment-to-zone assignment by graph cut and transformer voting (slide 4).

**Speaker notes.** Pick two questions and tell them as stories: the recloser join that returned zero, and the
feature model that did not beat overhead miles. The first shows we checked joins instead of trusting them; the
second shows we published a negative result and changed the model because of it (the posterior leans on observed zone
counts). The plan was explicit: `PLAN.md` milestones M1–M3 each had done-criteria (premise→path ≥ 99.9 %, zone
customers summing to circuit customers, ≥ 99 % of damage rows attributed) and `data/data_report.md` is the record that
they were met. If a judge asks "what did you not get to ask", answer: whether tie switches and back-feeds change the
AND-restoration assumption (`results/risk_validation.md:59`), and whether per-customer outage records would calibrate
the probability (`:58`).

## Slide 4 · Two knowledge areas and the hardest piece (Breadth and complexity: two areas, hardest part, 10×)

- **Distribution protection engineering** (a fault inside a protective zone trips that device and everything
  downstream; service needs every upstream zone repaired) **× operations research** (Horn's out-tree scheduling, list
  scheduling on K crews, brute-force reference ≤ 7 tasks) gives the new idea: an ordering that minimises
  customer-outage hours instead of repairs per hour. Supporting areas: statistics (Poisson regression with an
  exposure offset, gamma-Poisson empirical Bayes, dispersion α_line = 0.119 and α_equipment = 0.172,
  `results/risk_validation.md:19`), GIS (WGS84 vs Web Mercator, feet vs metres, `data/data_report.md:48`), and
  emergency-preparedness communication (safety items cite ESFI and utility downed-line guidance, CDC, FoodSafety.gov /
  USDA FSIS / FDA, Ready.gov, the Red Cross and the NWS, `concierge/guidance.py`, `SOURCE_LABELS`; full citations in
  `ASSUMPTIONS.md` C4 and the README).
- **Most complicated piece:** attributing 62,788 primary segments (`data/data_report.md:13`) to 7,190 zones (`:23`).
  Cut the structure graph at device locations, vote with the transformers inside each piece (57,735 segments), use
  the lowest common ancestor of the boundary devices for pieces without transformers (3,254), device links (1,379),
  nearest transformer (416), breaker default (737); 259 unassigned, 992 cross-circuit leftovers dropped (`:74`).
  99.59 % of segments carry a zone (`:75`) and overhead miles are conserved on 110 of 110 circuits (`:76`); the tree
  reaches depth 10 (`:67`) and 45 zones had conflicting parents resolved by majority (`:65`).
- **What changes at 10× (Measured on one machine, `results/scale_probe.md`):** Horn ordering 0.78 ms at 209 tasks →
  1.24 ms at 2,090 (`:14`, `:20`); one objective evaluation 0.83 ms → 4.14 ms (`:15`, `:21`); one full swap pass
  0.16 s measured → 8.65 s projected, not run (`:16`, `:22`). At the app's setting (swap pass capped at 1,500
  evaluations under a 1 s ceiling) that is 1,204 evaluations (≈ 5.8 passes, 0.96 % gain) today versus 218 evaluations
  (0.10 of a pass, 0.00 % gain) at 10× (`:17`, `:23`): the swap pass is the quadratic part and the cap and ceiling
  already truncate it. The probe also records that on this 4-CPU machine the 1 s ceiling, not the cap, ends the pass
  on the largest demo circuit, so that circuit's displayed schedule is machine-dependent there (`:17`).
- **What changes in the model at 10×:** per-circuit sharding (every circuit is an independent tree and solve), a
  shared crew pool with travel between circuits instead of crews dedicated to one circuit, and tie switches that turn
  an AND-chain into the earlier of two paths and break the out-tree assumption Horn's optimality rests on
  (`results/scale_probe.md:33`). The nearest-premise lookup is a linear scan (0.58 ms over 16,145 premises, `:27`)
  that a spatial index replaces.
- **Engineering practice:** stdlib shapefile reader (no GDAL), parquet tables, one `Scenario` object, 3-label tagging
  enforced in text, unit tests for ILM-once, AND-semantics, no double counting and brute-force parity (`PLAN.md`, M5
  done-criteria).

**Speaker notes.** Lead with the two areas and the one sentence that joins them ("protection zones make restoration an
AND over a path; scheduling theory has an optimal order for exactly that tree"). For the hardest piece, show diagram
(a) and point at the topology box: the problem is that the GIS layers do not say which zone a wire belongs to, and
getting it wrong double-counts exposure, so conservation of overhead miles on all 110 circuits is the test we trust.
For 10×, quote only `results/scale_probe.md` figures and say they are Measured on the machine named at `:4`; the
interactive journey costs 1.21 s for the Observed set and 2.58 s / 4.56 s for Simulated draws at 1× / 3× (`:24-26`),
and the cost is per circuit, so a 10× territory is 10× as many independent journeys, not one journey 10× slower
(`:31`).

## Slide 5 · Impact and feasibility (Impact: who it helps, user needs, feedback)

- **Who:** residential customers asking the four questions (will I lose power, how long, what should I do, do I get
  alerts); declared life-support accounts, 113 in the extract (`data/data_report.md:55`, Observed), who get a voice
  call on every stage and a wellness check when the estimate exceeds 8 h (`concierge/alerts.py`, rule E3); dispatchers,
  who see three policies on identical inputs. Critical facilities are counted per zone without names or coordinates
  (`concierge/priority.py`).
- **Value, quantified and honest:** 51.5 % fewer customer-outage hours than FIFO and 9.6 % fewer than the
  customers-per-hour heuristic across 306 instances; weighted by affected customers, 58.0 % and 9.1 %
  (`results/benchmark_summary.md:9-10`, Simulated). Losses are listed, not hidden (3 of 306, `:40`).
- **Scale check against reality:** at every circuit's calibrated default crews the last customer is restored after a
  median of 1.5 days (10th–90th percentile 0.5–2.8); the organizer's day-granularity repair records for the same
  storm show report→repair gaps of 0–6 days for 99.6 % of 478 repaired reference-storm records in the loaded bundle
  (median 1 day, 90th percentile 4 days) (`results/benchmark_summary.md:56`). Partly by construction, so a scale
  check, not a validation.
- **Feasible to build on:** inputs are feeds AEP already keeps (ARCOS damage assessments, GIS layers, upline paths,
  the labor workbook); loading the demo bundle takes 0.23 s after 1.58 s of module imports, and one journey 1.21 s,
  on a 4-CPU machine (`results/scale_probe.md:13`, `:24`, Measured); the public demo runs from 17 parquet tables,
  5.6 MB in total (`data/demo/README.md`, "Tables"), with anonymized ids and premise coordinates rounded to 0.001°, at
  most 71 m from the true point (`data/demo/README.md`, "Privacy").
- **Feedback and limits:** no customer feedback collected yet; say so. One reference storm, no per-customer outage
  records, the probability is a damage-intensity estimate rather than a calibrated forecast, tie switches are not
  modelled (`results/risk_validation.md:56-59`). The territory median P(outage) at 1× is 39.2 % with a 90th
  percentile of 91.5 % (`:49`), against an Observed anchor of 29.4 % of pathed customers with Feb-2025 damage upstream
  (`:52`).

**Speaker notes.** Answer "who does it help" with the life-support example first (334630102: wellness-check escalation
active at the default crew count, `data/demo/examples.json:28-32`); it is the clearest case where timing and channel
change for a declared need and nothing is inferred (`ASSUMPTIONS.md`, C2). Then the dispatcher value (customer-outage
hours) and the customer value (a range with reasons, not a single time). Be direct about feedback: none from
customers; what we would do first is a 15-minute comprehension test of the four messages with 3–5 people and record
which sentence they misread. If asked about adoption: the alert rules are Assumed (C1), AEP's own policy would replace
them, and the Observed / Assumed / Simulated tagging is designed so that swap is visible, not silent.

## Slide 6 · What failed and how we pivoted (Navigating challenges: one thing that did not work; how you tested)

- **The recloser join returned zero.** Matching path tokens to the recloser layer by id: 0.0 % of 322 tokens; by
  location: 100.0 % (`data/data_report.md:41-43`). Pivot: reclosers match by location, fuses by id (`ASSUMPTIONS.md`,
  A2). Same pattern for circuit keys: the anonymized id disagreed between layers, so 14,397 primary and 20,553
  secondary rows were remapped to the transformer layer's key, leaving 0 unresolved damage keys
  (`data/data_report.md:62`, `:83`).
- **The feature-rich risk model did not beat overhead miles.** Leave-one-circuit-out Spearman 0.658 vs 0.644, MAE
  8.83 vs 8.63 (`results/risk_validation.md:27-28`). Pivot: keep the regression only as a prior and let each zone's
  own Feb-2025 count move the posterior (373 zones with line damage; posterior sum 919 vs observed 881, `:21`);
  publish the negative result in the app and the README instead of hiding it.
- **The customer-benefit greedy looked good until we tested AND-coupling.** Pivot to Horn's tree-merge: 9.6 % fewer
  customer-outage hours on average, lost on 3 of 306 instances, worst −1.6 % (`results/benchmark_summary.md:10`,
  `:40`). The swap pass we nearly cut adds only 1.39 % on average but up to 20.42 % (`:50`), so it stayed, capped at
  1,500 evaluations in the app (`concierge/config.py`, `SWAP_EVALUATION_CAP`, Assumed L9) so the schedule is the same
  on any machine that reaches the cap inside the 1 s ceiling; the scale probe shows the largest demo circuit does not
  on a 4-CPU machine (`results/scale_probe.md:17`), which is reported, not hidden.
- **Our own number hygiene failed once.** An early `scripts/run_benchmark.py` carried "0–6 days for 97 % of records"
  as a typed string, against our rule that every figure comes from a script. Pivot: the gap is now computed from the
  loaded bundle (99.6 % of 478 repaired reference-storm records within 0–6 days, median 1 day, 90th percentile 4
  days, max 80, `results/benchmark_summary.md:56`) and written to `results/benchmark_headline.json` with its label.
- **A pre-judging audit of the M7 submission found judge-visible defects, and we fixed the model rather than the
  wording:** the crew start delay was added in the solver and again at display time → schedules are solved from hour
  0 and the delay is added once, with a test (`concierge/evidence.py`, `changed_after_testing`, row "Crew start
  delay"); one premise whose path names zones outside
  the demo bundle crashed the page → listed explicitly in `data/demo/README.md` ("Coverage") and handled; example
  premises and demo steps were hand-picked → now chosen by `scripts/pick_examples.py` against written criteria
  (`data/demo/examples.json:4-11`).

**Speaker notes.** Tell the recloser story first because it is concrete and the before/after numbers are stark (0 %
→ 100 %). Then the risk model, because it is the one where the honest answer was "the clever feature does not help":
we kept it as a prior and said so on screen. For "how did you test", list the tests that gate each milestone
(`PLAN.md`, M5 done-criteria): brute-force parity on ≤ 7-task instances (60 of 64 at the optimum, max gap 9.15 %,
`results/benchmark_summary.md:51`), AND-semantics and no-double-counting tests on the scheduler, ILM-applied-once on
labor, and the consistency test that one `Scenario` change moves schedule, range, explanation and message together.
Close with the audit: finding the delay double count after the deploy was uncomfortable, and the fix was in the solver
and its tests, not in the message text. Before the interview, confirm on `main` that each audit fix in the last bullet
is merged; if one is not, say "found and in progress" rather than "fixed".
